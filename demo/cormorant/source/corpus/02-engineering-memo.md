# Engineering findings: primary bus failure, DSV Cormorant

To the Dive Operations Officer, 19 November. From the Vehicle Systems group.

We have completed teardown of the primary power distribution assembly recovered
from dive 214. The failure originated in the starboard penetrator block, at the
junction where the main bus passes from the pressure sphere into the wet bay.

The penetrator uses a compression seal against a machined titanium seat. On
disassembly we found the seat scored along roughly a third of its circumference.
The scoring is consistent with a hard particle trapped during the seal
replacement carried out in the August maintenance period. Under pressure the
compression seal deformed into the scoring, opening a path for seawater to reach
the conductor. Resistance across the junction rose as salt deposits accumulated,
which accounts for the two transient dropouts before the full failure.

We want to be plain about the sequence. This was not a sudden failure. The
vehicle gave two warnings, seventeen minutes apart, and the second one took
propulsion offline for two seconds at 1,610 metres. Under the procedures in
force at the time, neither warning obliged the crew to abort. We think that is
the finding that matters, more than the seal itself.

The August maintenance period was carried out at sea rather than in the shore
facility, following the schedule compression agreed after the Kestrel Rise
survey was brought forward. Seal replacement at sea is permitted under the
maintenance manual. It is performed in the wet bay, which is not a clean
environment.

Recommendations. First, penetrator seal work should be restricted to the shore
facility. Second, any transient on the primary bus exceeding one second should
be a mandatory abort. Third, the reserve bus should carry sufficient power for
station-keeping, so that a vehicle on reserve is not obliged to surface blind
under whatever current it happens to be in.
