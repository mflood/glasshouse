# Pilot's log, dive 214

Ines Okonkwo, transcribed from the voice record.

0942. Away clean. Sea state two. Tobias has the descent.

1015. Passing 800. Water is very clear today, clearer than the last three dives.
We are seeing the thermocline as a shimmer rather than a wall.

1114. Sixteen ten. We had a flicker on the main bus, under half a second.
Everything came back on its own. Logged it. Tobias says he has seen worse on the
older vehicles. Continuing.

1131. Sixteen ten again, holding for the survey line. Second flicker and this
one had teeth — two seconds, and it took the forward thrusters and the main
lights with it. We were dark and drifting for those two seconds. Amara did not
say anything, which is how I know it was bad. Everything came back.

I want to say here that I thought about calling it. The procedure does not
require an abort for a transient. I read the criteria twice on the way down and
they are written around things that have already happened. A flicker is not a
listed event. I decided that twice is a pattern and I would call it if there was
a third.

1158. Eighteen forty-four. Third one, and it did not come back. Main bus gone.
We are on reserve. Life support good, comms good, no thrust.

1204. Weights away. We are going up.

1230. Passing 1100. Steady, about twenty-two a minute. Amara is doing the
breathing exercise. CO2 is climbing but inside limits.

1315. Passing 300. I can see the light change.

1331. Surfaced. We are a long way east of the ship. Nothing to do about that
without thrusters — you go up where the water takes you.

1452. Aboard. Everyone walked out.

Note for the debrief: the criteria are wrong. Not the seal, whatever they find.
The criteria. We were given two warnings and told we did not need to listen.
