# Review board findings: dive 214

Adopted 8 December. The board comprised three members, none of whom belonged to
the Vehicle Systems group or to Dive Operations.

The board accepts the Engineering account of the mechanism. A scored penetrator
seat admitted seawater to the starboard bus junction; accumulating salt deposits
raised resistance until the bus failed. We do not restate that analysis here.

The board's concern is different. Cormorant gave two warnings before it failed,
and the procedures in force did not require the crew to act on either. The
second transient took propulsion offline for two seconds at 1,610 metres. Had
the full failure occurred at that moment rather than twenty-seven minutes later,
the vehicle would have been in the same condition it was ultimately in — on
reserve power, without propulsion — but 234 metres shallower and closer to the
ridge wall. The board regards the outcome of dive 214 as substantially a matter
of where the vehicle happened to be.

On the maintenance decision, the board finds that performing penetrator seal
work at sea was permitted, was not unusual, and had been done four times in the
preceding two years without incident. The board declines to treat the August
work as an error. It notes instead that a procedure permitting work in a
non-clean environment, with no post-work verification of the seat, tolerates a
failure mode it has no way to detect.

The board makes two findings. The programme's schedule compression, agreed in
June, removed the shore maintenance window that would otherwise have applied.
And the abort criteria in force were written around events rather than around
warnings, which is why two warnings produced no obligation.

The board endorses all three of the Engineering recommendations and adds a
fourth: that abort criteria be reviewed by someone outside the operating group,
annually.
