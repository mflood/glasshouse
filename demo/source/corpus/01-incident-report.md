# Preliminary incident report: DSV Cormorant, dive 214

Filed by the Dive Operations Officer, 3 November.

At 09:42 local time on 2 November, the deep submergence vehicle Cormorant
departed the support ship Halvard Lyng for a routine survey of the Kestrel Rise,
a basalt ridge in the eastern basin. The dive plan called for four hours at a
working depth of 1,850 metres, with two hours of transit either side. Aboard
were pilot Ines Okonkwo, co-pilot Tobias Reiner, and observer Dr Amara Sell.

Descent was normal. At 11:14, passing 1,610 metres, the vehicle reported a
transient dropout on the primary power bus lasting approximately 400
milliseconds. The crew logged it and continued. A second dropout at 11:31 lasted
just over two seconds and took the forward thrusters and the primary lighting
array offline. Both recovered without intervention.

At 11:58, at a depth of 1,844 metres, the primary bus failed entirely. The
vehicle transferred to the reserve bus as designed. The reserve bus carries life
support, communications, and the ballast release, but not propulsion. Cormorant
was at that point neutrally buoyant and drifting at approximately 0.3 knots on
the prevailing current.

The crew followed the standard procedure and released the descent weights at
12:04. Ascent began at roughly 22 metres per minute. Cormorant surfaced at 13:31
approximately 4.1 nautical miles east of the Halvard Lyng and was recovered at
14:52. No injuries were sustained. Dr Sell reported a headache consistent with
the elevated carbon dioxide observed in the final forty minutes of ascent.

The vehicle was recovered intact. Initial inspection found no hull breach and no
water ingress in the pressure sphere. The cause of the bus failure was not
determined at the time of filing and is referred to Engineering.

This report is preliminary. It records what was observed and does not attempt to
assign a cause.
